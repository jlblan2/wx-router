      Vessel

      Sail: legs that would point closer to the wind than this, once there's that much wind, are avoided, so the route reaches across the wind instead of beating into it.

      Air draft is the height from the waterline to the top of the mast or arch. Routes only pass under a fixed bridge whose charted clearance is at least 1 ft more; fixed bridges with no charted clearance are avoided.

      Keep off land: legs stay at least this far from the coastline, so a route doesn't clip a point, a reef edge or a cay the chart data places a little off. The first and last 2 nm at your start, destination and waypoints are exempt, so harbours can still be reached.

      Routes stay out of water shallower than the minimum depth, using NOAA bathymetry. Those depths are measured from about high water, not chart datum, and small shoals can be missed, so leave a margin over your draft (draft plus 3 ft is a sensible start).

      Maximum seas allowed (ft)

      Set by where the seas strike the boat: eight points around the vessel, each covering 45°.

      Maximum winds allowed (kt)

      Sustained wind, set by where it comes from relative to the bow.

      Steep, short seas

      Avoid any area where the waves are higher than this and come this close together, whatever direction they come from.

      Cruising hours

      For example 18:00 until 08:00 keeps you off the water overnight. Times are this phone's local time. Every departure must arrive before the no-cruise hours start; on longer trips, add your own waypoints where you can anchor, and the route stops there overnight and goes on the next morning.

      Weather data

      The free Open-Meteo service allows about 10,000 forecast points a day and 600 a minute, and a long route uses several hundred. With a paid Open-Meteo subscription key here, Wx Router uses the subscriber servers, which have no daily or per-minute limit. The key stays on this phone.
